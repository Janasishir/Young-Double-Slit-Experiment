Young's Double-Slit Experiment: Theory

Young's double-slit experiment demonstrates the wave nature of light. A coherent, monochromatic source illuminates two narrow slits. The waves emerging from the slits overlap on a screen and produce alternating bright and dark fringes.

1. Experimental arrangement

  1. Use a monochromatic coherent light source, such as a laser.
  2. Illuminate two narrow slits of width a, separated by centre-to-centre distance d.
  3. Place the observation screen at distance L from the slits.
  4. Observe the intensity as a function of the screen coordinate y.

2. Interference and diffraction equation

Combined intensity:

I(θ) = I₀ [sin(β) / β]² cos²(α)

α = πd sin(θ) / λ

β = πa sin(θ) / λ

sin(θ) = y / √(L² + y²)

3. Meaning of the terms

  • λ: wavelength of the light.
  • d: separation between the centres of the two slits.
  • a: width of each slit.
  • L: distance between the double slit and the screen.
  • y: position measured from the central axis on the screen.
  • θ: diffraction/interference observation angle.

4. Fringe spacing

Fringe spacing near the centre:

Δy ≈ λL / d

Increasing wavelength or screen distance increases the fringe spacing. Increasing slit separation decreases the fringe spacing. The finite slit width produces a diffraction envelope that controls the overall brightness.

6. Model limitations

This is an educational visualization based on the Fraunhofer approximation and idealized coherent illumination. The coloured screen is a visual representation of normalized intensity, not a full electromagnetic simulation.
